The goal

### Summary

Build laps through repeated spinner rotations and upper-playfield scoring, then preserve the end-of-ball collect. Late-ball lap value can outweigh early scoring, so favor a controllable side shot and a safe lit-saucer rebound over direct bumper contact.

### Steps

- Plunge for a top lane or lit saucer that keeps the ball among the bumpers.

- Shoot either side spinner and aim through it rather than directly into a bumper face.

- Use gentle nudges to turn upper rebounds into another spinner pass or a lit saucer entry.

- On later balls, repeat the safest spinner route and avoid lower-risk detours that threaten the built lap collect.

- Ease off the cabinet before the drain so the completed laps score at end of ball.

## Full strategy

### Overview

Snow Queen and Snow Derby turn playfield scoring into lap advances and an end-of-ball lap award. Every 10 points adds one advance, every 50 adds five, and 10 advances complete a lap. Shoot the two spinners up the sides, nudge upper action toward lit saucers, and protect the late balls because their lap value can rise from 50 to 100 and 200.

### Simple Overview

In Snow Queen or Snow Derby, shoot the side spinners and keep the ball in the upper bumpers. Every 10 points adds one advance; 10 advances make a lap. Favor lit top saucers, especially on the last two balls when the installed five-ball setup can double and then quadruple the 50-point lap value.

### Builds Toward

A large late-ball lap collect

Steps

- Use spinners and 50-point features to add advances quickly.

- Convert each 10 advances into a lap.

- Protect the ball and collect the installed late-ball lap value.

### Machine Setup

- Identify Snow Queen versus two-player Snow Derby, confirm three- or five-ball play, lap maximum, and the per-ball lap values shown by the installed feature adjustment.

- Test both spinners, the rotating rollunder target, all top saucers and kickouts, advance and lap units, late-ball value lamps, bumpers, end-of-ball collect, and tilt behavior.
