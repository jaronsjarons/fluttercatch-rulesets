### Summary

Start with the center saucer, then bank shots across the colored drops. Finish green and red first on 2001 because their lights improve return lanes; the other colors improve outlanes. Avoid the four central stand-ups unless their configured award is worth the rebound.

### Steps

- On 2001, plunge the center saucer for 300 before any colored saucer has been lit.

- Bank controlled shots across one colored five-drop group instead of shooting the central stand-ups.

- Finish green or red first to light its matching saucer and return lane for 300.

- Complete the configured three or four color groups for Special; on Dimension or Galaxie, follow the paired-color WOW and Add-A-Ball lamps instead.

## Full strategy

### Overview

2001, Dimension, and Galaxie share four colored five-target groups and five upper saucers, but their awards differ. On 2001, completing a color lights its matching saucer and lane from 50 to 300; completing three or four colors lights Special according to setup. Dimension and Galaxie convert paired color completions into Add-A-Balls, 1,000-point bumpers, and WOW targets. In either version, bank shots across the target faces are safer than the central stand-ups.

### Simple Overview

In 2001, shoot the colored drop groups and finish one color at a time. Each completed color lights its matching upper saucer and lane for 300 instead of 50; complete the configured three or four colors for Special. Dimension and Galaxie use Add-A-Ball and WOW awards instead, so identify the title before following the final award route.

### Machine Setup

- Identify 2001, Dimension, or Galaxie before play. On 2001, confirm whether three or four completed color sets light Special; on Dimension or Galaxie, confirm Add-A-Ball and WOW conversion and which completed side resets when the ball leaves play.

- Test all twenty colored drops, each group-completion relay, five saucers and kickouts, matching colored lanes, Special or WOW lamps, Dimension/Galaxie bumper upgrades, central stand-ups, target resets, and the target-to-sling return geometry.
